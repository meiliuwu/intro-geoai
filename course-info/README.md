# Course information

**Introduction to Geospatial Artificial Intelligence** · 20 credits · MSc Geospatial Data Science and AI (core), and an option on several other GES Master's programmes · University of Glasgow · from 2026–27

## Aims

The course equips students with the conceptual understanding and practical skills to design and implement GeoAI workflows for real-world geospatial problems across environmental, urban and societal domains. It introduces the historical roots and methodological foundations of GeoAI within GIScience, covering spatially explicit deep learning, graph-based models, representation learning and geospatial foundation models. Through student-led reading, hands-on labs and a final project, students learn to select, implement and evaluate GeoAI methods with open-source tools and reproducible code, and to reflect critically on the ethical, legal and societal implications of GeoAI, such as bias, privacy and sustainability.

## Intended learning outcomes

By the end of the course students will be able to:

1. Explain the conceptual foundations, historical development and current research agenda of GeoAI within GIScience, including its distinctive contributions and limitations.
2. Justify appropriate GeoAI methods and data representations for a given scenario, taking account of spatial autocorrelation, scale, uncertainty and other constraints of geospatial data.
3. Evaluate GeoAI models with spatially aware designs and metrics, such as spatial cross-validation.
4. Communicate results to technical and non-technical audiences in visual, oral and written formats.
5. Develop end-to-end GeoAI workflows that ingest, preprocess and model different types of geospatial data (Earth observation imagery, vector features, networks and location-based data) with state-of-the-art open-source toolkits.
6. Assess the ethical, legal and societal implications of GeoAI (bias, fairness, privacy, energy cost and reproducibility) and practical ways to mitigate them.
7. Build a feasible and reproducible GeoAI project, with a proposal presentation, a shared code repository and a project report.

## Timetable pattern

| Weeks | Sessions |
| --- | --- |
| 1–7 | 2-hour lecture and 2-hour computer lab each week, on the same topic and the same day |
| 8 | 4-hour session of project proposal presentations and peer feedback |
| 9–10 | 4-hour project lab each week |

## Prerequisites

Basic GIS knowledge (for example *Principles of GIS*) and prior programming experience, ideally in Python. Machine-learning experience helps but is not required: each lab has core steps everyone completes and optional stretch tasks.

## Assessment

| Component | Weight | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Essay of up to 2,000 words on a theme from the lectures | 40% | After Week 7 |
| Short oral presentation of the project proposal | 10% | Week 8 |
| Project report of up to 2,000 words applying the lab techniques | 50% | After Week 10 |

See [assessment](../assessment/) for essay themes and project guidance.
