# Week 3 · Environmental monitoring

**Big question:** can we trust a data-driven model where we have no observations?

## Core readings

1. Reichstein, M., Camps-Valls, G., Stevens, B., Jung, M., Denzler, J., Carvalhais, N. and Prabhat (2019) Deep learning and process understanding for data-driven Earth system science. *Nature* 566, 195–204. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-0912-1
   *Why:* the case for, and limits of, deep learning in Earth system science, and the idea of hybrid models.
2. Nearing, G. et al. (2024) Global prediction of extreme floods in ungauged watersheds. *Nature* 627, 559–563. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07145-1
   *Why:* an AI flood-forecasting system tested on rivers it has never seen.
3. Meyer, H. and Pebesma, E. (2021) Predicting into unknown space? Estimating the area of applicability of spatial prediction models. *Methods in Ecology and Evolution* 12(9), 1620–1633. https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.13650
   *Why:* how to tell where a spatial model's predictions can be trusted.

## Annotation prompts

- Reichstein et al. list several challenges for deep learning in Earth science. Which one does Nearing et al. tackle most directly?
- What makes a catchment "ungauged", and why is it harder to predict than a gauged one?
- How would you compute an area of applicability for a model trained only on Scottish rivers?
